# Producing Surveys in Plain Language or Easy Read

::: info
Using Plain Language and Easy Read makes information more accessible for everyone. It presents content simply, directly, and clearly.
:::

<lite-youtube videoid="ol5tZ2hnUgE"></lite-youtube>

*Introduction to Easy Read and Plain Language produced by [Open Collaboration for Cognitive Accessibility](https://openaccessibility.ca/).*

## Summary

- **Universal Accessibility:** Plain Language and Easy Read present content in a straightforward, understandable way. They benefit all respondents, not only those who require accessibility accommodations.
- **Single Survey Approach:** To maximize inclusion and maintain data integrity, we recommend designing one survey using Plain Language as the baseline, with Easy Read mode enabled for those who benefit from visual and simplified text support.
- **Professional Collaboration:** Converting complex text into Easy Read or Plain Language benefits from trained specialists and co-design or usability testing with persons with intellectual disabilities.
- **Integrated Tooling:** Accessible Surveys allows you to author standard content and Easy Read visual aids within the same survey structure, preserving data comparability across all respondents.

## Understanding Plain Language and Easy Read

While both approaches share the goal of clear communication, they serve different needs and follow distinct conventions:

### Plain Language

Plain Language is a direct, clear, and concise style of writing. It aims to ensure that the audience can understand information the first time they read or hear it. Plain Language avoids convoluted phrasing, bureaucratic jargon, and unnecessary acronyms, while assuming that respondents can read at a standard reading level.

Key characteristics:

- Short, active-voice sentences.
- Common, everyday vocabulary.
- Direct addresses to the reader ("you").
- Clear headings, lists, and visual hierarchy.

### Easy Read

Easy Read is an accessible, alternative format designed specifically to support persons with intellectual disabilities or cognitive difficulties. It pairs short, simplified sentences with supporting images, illustrations, or photographs that reinforce the meaning of each statement.

Key characteristics:

- One main idea per sentence.
- Simple, everyday words with clear explanations for necessary technical terms.
- Supporting images or symbols placed alongside each text block or question.
- Generous typography, ample whitespace, and clear section demarcation.

### Who Benefits from Easy Read?

Easy Read is essential for persons with intellectual disabilities, and it also benefits:

- People reading in a second or non-native language.
- People with learning difficulties such as dyslexia or dyspraxia.
- Older persons experiencing cognitive fatigue or visual decline.
- Children and youth.
- Anyone reading about a complex or unfamiliar topic under time pressure.

## Why Plain Language and Easy Read Matter

Adopting Plain Language and Easy Read provides measurable benefits for survey research:

1. **Enhanced Data Quality:** Clear, unambiguous questions reduce misunderstandings and invalid responses.
2. **Reduced Survey Fatigue:** Respondents can process questions faster and with less cognitive strain, leading to higher completion rates.
3. **Respect for Diversity:** Accessible formats welcome respondents who are frequently excluded from public consultations and research.

## Legal and Policy Context

The [United Nations Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)](https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities.html) establishes accessibility and access to information as fundamental human rights:

- **Article 9 (Accessibility):** Calls upon State Parties to ensure persons with disabilities have access to information and communications technologies and systems.
- **Article 21 (Freedom of Expression and Opinion, and Access to Information):** Requires providing information intended for the general public in accessible formats and technologies, including easy-to-read formats and sign languages.

In addition, the United Nations General Assembly and Human Rights Council have adopted resolutions promoting easy-to-understand communication as a critical accessibility standard. National legislations (such as the UK Equality Act 2010 and the European Accessibility Act) reinforce the obligation for public bodies and service providers to communicate accessibly.

## Choosing Your Approach

::: tip
An Easy Read survey is not merely a Plain Language survey with decorative images.
:::

Easy Read and Plain Language are complementary formats structured for different user needs:

- **Surveys targeting persons with intellectual disabilities:** Must be provided in Easy Read.
- **Surveys targeting a broad, inclusive audience:** Use Plain Language as the baseline for all text, and activate the platform's **Easy Read** mode so respondents can switch to illustrated, simplified presentations at will.

### Architectural Recommendations

- **Maintain Baseline Comparability:** Do not create two separate, diverging surveys (one in standard text and another in Easy Read). Running two separate surveys risks asking different questions, which invalidates statistical comparisons.
- **Use Platform Modes:** Accessible Surveys enables you to attach Easy Read media and simplified labels to the same survey items. The underlying question codes remain identical, ensuring responses are stored in the same dataset regardless of the mode used.
- **Support Variable Length:** If your survey is comprehensive, consider organizing it so essential core questions appear first. Respondents can then be asked if they wish to continue or submit early, allowing individuals to dedicate the time that suits them without being excluded.
- **Section Introductions:** For longer surveys, consider adding a brief introductory video or plain-language summary page at the start of each section to provide context and reduce anxiety.

## Step-by-Step Production Process

### 1. Preparing Accessible Content

- **Write in Plain Language from the Start:** Draft questions using simple, direct sentences. Avoid idioms, abbreviations, and double negatives.
- **Engage Trained Specialists:** Partner with organizations specializing in Plain Language or Easy Read adaptations. Ensure that persons with intellectual disabilities are involved in reviewing and testing the content.
- **Verify Meaning:** Review adapted questions against your research objectives to confirm that the simplified text preserves the original research intent.
- **Provide In-Line Definitions:** For unfamiliar terms that cannot be simplified further, use the platform's tooltip and helper text features to display plain-language definitions on focus.
- **Validate Translations:** When translating into other languages, test the translated Easy Read version with native speakers who have intellectual disabilities, as plain-language nuances do not always translate directly.

### 2. Sourcing and Designing Easy Read Images

- **Purpose-Driven Visuals:** Every image must directly support the meaning of the question or option. Avoid purely decorative graphics, abstract metaphors, or ambiguous icons.
- **Human-Centric Imagery:** Photographs or clear illustrations showing people performing actions are generally easier to understand than abstract symbols.
- **Consistent Aspect Ratio and Style:** Use consistent ratios (square 1:1 or 4:3) and visual styles across all survey items to avoid visual confusion.
- **Respect Copyright:** Ensure you hold the appropriate licensing or rights to reuse illustrations in subsequent survey iterations.

## Resources and Standards

### Standards and Guidelines

- **[Information for All (European Standards for Making Information Easy to Read and Understand)](https://www.inclusion-europe.eu/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/EN_Information_for_all.pdf):** Published by Inclusion Europe, outlining visual, textual, and layout rules.
- **[Listen Include Respect Guidelines](https://www.listenincluderespect.com/surveys):** Co-produced by Inclusion International and Down Syndrome International, providing guidance on inclusive consultations and surveys.
- **[Easy English vs Plain English Guide](https://centreforinclusivedesign.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Easy-English-vs-Plain-English_accessible.pdf):** Center for Inclusive Design overview of distinction and implementation.

### Service Providers and Organizations

The following organizations provide expertise, adaptation services, and testing with persons with intellectual disabilities:

- **[CHANGE](https://www.changepeople.org/):** Human rights organization offering bespoke Easy Read and accessible media production.
- **[Inclusion Europe](https://www.inclusion-europe.eu/):** European association of persons with intellectual disabilities and their families, with member organizations across Europe providing Easy Read translations.
- **[People First New Zealand / Make It Easy](https://www.makeiteasy.org.nz/):** Disabled Persons Organization offering translation into Easy Read formats.
- **[Easy on the i](https://www.learningdisabilityservice-leeds.nhs.uk/easy-on-the-i/):** Information design service by the Leeds and York NHS Trust with a large library of public image assets.
- **[Open Collaboration for Cognitive Accessibility](https://openaccessibility.ca/):** Offers accessibility review and cognitive accessibility testing services.
- **[Plain English Campaign](https://www.plainenglish.co.uk/):** Professional editing and accreditation for plain English texts.
